I am,Out The Other Side.

It didn’t have to be that hard — but I now know it was just market research for this moment. This is my journey, corrected, for those still trapped and still relapsing.

CGL & CRS — Cambridgeshire

A year ago, once I was out, I realised I had to build a life with some balance in it — working in recovery, and supporting my own in the process. Level 2 Counselling Skills was never going to be enough for a career in therapy, so a few weeks after the medication started I had my first idea: a private, one-to-one, mentored “digital rehab”. I was going to solve ADHD-driven addiction by teaching people how I got out.

Then I learnt more about the system, and the systems within it, and the plan changed. I shifted from trying to build the lifeboat to get myself through recovery, to exposing — and ultimately trying to build — the river itself. I realised the river had problems of its own — the walls between services among them — and that what was required was a separate layer: secure, consent-led, tokenised infrastructure that handles the communication and the holding that is so desperately needed.

Before any of this, before the medication, I could barely open my laptop — let alone design the technical requirement around a medical intervention that already exists, and the infrastructure to hold it. After meeting Cambridgeshire Public Health back in April, I spent the time educating myself on the landscape and meeting the people who shape it — and, bit by bit, it grew: into infrastructure, into a business that is ready to move, and into a very real opportunity to make a difference. That is the OTOS Continuity Layer.

Since then the news has hotted up. Statements have been made — the NHS’s own Taskforce among them — and everything I was predicting is exactly on course.

You both know I have been very well. A little quiet, maybe — but only because I wanted to take the time and get this right. I have been laser-focused, something I thought I would never feel again, and here it is. I want your honest read, I want your help, and there is a lot I would rather say in the room than on a page — so I have written this to be read before we meet, not instead of it.

Shared in confidence. This document, the design it describes and the method behind it are my own work. I am sharing it with you for your read and your view — not for it to be taken forward by anyone else. If any part of it is useful, I would far rather we did it together.

01

Thank you first.

The thread held once. It held because people held it.

You both saw me at my worst, and recently, at my best. But this “best” presentation of me is only possible because two people here held the thread when nobody had to. Two people at CGL held the thread for me. A letter from CGL — an email, written in her role — was one of the two things a psychiatrist needed before he would treat me, and it arrived unprompted, the same day. Then that psychiatrist rang CGL to check everything, and one of you took the call — a call nobody had to take. I only found out weeks later. For the first time in this whole story, the thread held.

There were many contributing factors, experiences and services in my journey, and I understand why there is an “ADHD crisis”. But the magical, life-changing thing that happened to me is this: the ability to truly handle and survive life — the addiction parked, under control, no longer running things. I felt it only after my ADHD was properly medicated. I want to make that happen, quickly, for people who are probably trapped where I was — and they are certainly showing up in the evidence. That is exactly the problem I am trying to solve. If the idea works, it could be massive. I need help trying, and I would rather solve it with the people who do the work than around them.

02

The pattern you watch walk through the door

Named in writing, by a consultant psychiatrist, about me.

It is generally agreed that addiction is rooted in trauma; trauma is treated by mental-health teams, while a different service picks up the presentation. But there is something I believe — and, with support, can prove — is being missed. For myself and a significant, high-cost, identifiable group of adults, untreated ADHD is driving, recycling and fanning the fire of the addiction, regardless of origin. The chaos, collapses and crises are just what arrives in view.

Self-medication works — until it doesn’t. Alcohol or substances can do what an untreated ADHD brain has been trying to do all along: supply motivation, sustain attention, create focus and cut the noise. It gave me the initial spark, the energy, the focus, and relief from a low-grade mental friction I didn’t know I was suffering with. It feels like function, not fun. That is not a moral failure. It is a neurological system finding the one thing that seems to fix it. And then it crosses the line into dependence, floods a reward system that turns down its own sensitivity and erodes the very systems recovery depends on.

In April 2026 an independent consultant psychiatrist wrote to the CPFT Adult ADHD Team about my care and named the pattern in one sentence:

“Adult ADHD previously complicated with excess alcohol use to self-medicate inadequately managed ADHD symptoms.”

Read the order of it. Not ADHD plus alcohol, treated separately. Not alcohol with ADHD as a comorbidity. ADHD — complicated by self-medication. That letter is about me. It proves nothing about anything I have built. It proves that the thing you see every week has a name, in writing.

Someone who does this work told me recovery is ninety per cent mindset. I agree with her. But to get to that ninety per cent, the mind has to be at a hundred per cent working order in the first place — and with ADHD it isn’t. I am no doctor. I am someone it happened to, and I have sat in enough rooms to believe I am not the only one.

Founder experience is not population evidence and I am not presenting it as any.

03

How the system met me

It saw what was on fire. It never asked what was burning.

I reached CGL through a courtroom. Police and probation met the addiction long before anyone asked what was driving it. I was funded for residential rehab by CGL — after a year of turning up, and a panel that said yes — at a dual-diagnosis hospital staffed by remarkable people doing real care. On arrival my ADHD medication was removed, because it is a stimulant. I came out fully twelve-stepped and addiction-informed, with nothing underneath holding the brain that started all of it.

I had been diagnosed privately, and for a while I was on shared care with my GP. It was lost at a handoff when I changed surgery, and because the diagnosis was private rather than through the NHS route, no surgery would pick it back up. I was referred to the ADHD team in March 2021. Five years on, I am still waiting for the medication review. In that time I cycled through A&E, addiction services and mental-health teams, and no part of the system connected the loop.

Not because the people weren’t good. They were. But the model didn’t fit a brain that increasingly suspected it was being mis-described — and the clinical letter, years later, said so in writing.

When ADHD is left waiting and services run on rigid criteria, people do desperate things to qualify for help. That is a design problem, not a moral failing. None of this was your fault and none of it was your workload. It was never about the care you gave.

And the problem is not history. Diagnosed in 2020, referred in 2021, once on shared care — and to this day the letters A-D-H-D do not appear on my GP problem list. The system still cannot see the driver in me. That is why this cohort’s needs have to be taken over, and with urgency. They are specific, and they are very doable.

04

Why good work does not hold

The order of operations, and it is not optional.

Services quite reasonably respond to the addiction. But if the ADHD remains untouched, the work does not hold. Therapy, groups and keywork land on a brain that cannot sequence or retain them. People are being asked to recover using the exact executive functions their unmanaged ADHD has impaired.

Line up what successful addiction recovery requires against what unmanaged ADHD impairs — item for item.

CapacityUnmanaged ADHD impairsRecovery requires
Executive functionDifficulty planning, sequencing and self-regulatingDaily planning, structure, delaying gratification
Focus & attentionChronic distractibility on non-stimulating tasksDeep, continuous focus through long therapy and recovery work

Impulse controlReflexive, immediate urges; acting before the pausePausing, reflecting, resisting cravings in the moment
Emotional regulationDysregulation; distress arrives fast and overwhelmsTolerating distress, riding out craving without the substance

Read it as a model, not a verdict. The mapping is an explanatory framing drawn from the published account of ADHD and recovery; presentations vary and the alignment is directional. It is offered to show why this cohort struggles with standard recovery — not to diagnose, and not as a research finding. Whether it holds in a real local caseload is exactly what the report would establish.

The medication does not do the recovery work. It creates the conditions where it can finally land. I know that from the inside: the recovery work I did with you was excellent, and my brain could not use it yet.

Once the ADHD was treated, everything else we’d worked on finally started to hold.

So there is an order. One — the medical intervention. The brain gets what it needs to function. Two — then the re-wiring. The routines, the strategies, everything your service already does well, now landing on equipment that can hold it. Three — and then again, and again. Re-wiring is repetition, and repetition runs on days, not appointments.

So here is the real question, said plainly. Can the people already in addiction services be identified and fast-tracked through the ADHD process by the services that do that work — quicker than the waiting list, quicker than I was — and held the whole way, so that the addiction work can finally take? OTOS does not prioritise or fast-track anyone itself. The services do. The Layer holds people while it happens. Whether that is deliverable is exactly the question.

I know what the guidance says. Stabilise the substance misuse first; in uncontrolled and chaotic use, stimulants are not routinely recommended (CR235). The same guidance says that controlled misuse “should not completely preclude treatment, although careful monitoring will be required” — and that “when prescribed stimulants are used appropriately, there is little evidence of patients becoming dependent, and indeed treating ADHD may reduce the lifetime risk of developing a substance use disorder” (CR235 §11.1). I was stabilised first, more than once, and each time the driver went untreated. The sequence belongs to the clinicians. What I am asking is whether the person can be held between the steps, so they are not lost before the review.

05

The gaps — and why nobody owns them

Every service owns its episode. Nobody is accountable for the time in between.

The gaps are not where support stops. They are where the entire mechanism of recovery is supposed to happen — unsupervised, by the person least equipped to run it.

Nobody owns the risk in the gap. And the gap is not one gap. It starts the moment somebody first suspects ADHD, and it runs through the referral, the wait, the assessment, the medication review, and every handoff from one service into the next. At every one of those joins a person can go quiet with nobody downstream knowing.

I know how busy you are, and that CRS capacity is literally one phone call every two weeks — which I think everyone would agree simply isn’t enough connection for addiction and recovery work to hold, regardless of being ADHD.

It is an honest statement of a caseload, and nobody has ever pretended otherwise. The question is not how often a service can call. It is what happens in all the time in between — because that is where the repetition has to live, and the person living it is the one whose condition makes repetition hardest.

So I built something to be in them. Not a service. Not a caseworker. Nothing clinical. Continuity fills the gaps. Think of it as neuroplasticity for the system.

06

What I built to be in the gaps

A non-clinical layer that holds the thread between services.

To be clear, OTOS Continuity is infrastructure. It does not diagnose, prescribe, treat or replace anyone. OTOS surfaces the signals. The partner team makes every decision. Nothing changes in how you work; what changes is what becomes visible.

It starts at enrolment, not at handoff, and it is consent-led: nobody is on it without agreeing to be. One thread per person, with the person inside it. Services add to that person’s thread and can see it, per consent. Services do not connect to each other, and nobody’s clinical record is touched — each partner connects to Continuity, and it is a shared view of one journey, not a pipe between organisations. Every interaction is also a signal, and silence becomes visible instead of invisible.

What you would actually do with it — spelled out

You are not being asked to run anything. You are being given a system, and it does five plain things for a service like yours:

  1. You add a person to it. Someone on your caseload agrees to be on it; you enrol them from your own login, once. That is the whole onboarding.
  2. You talk to them more than you can now. Small, agreed messages go out to the person between your calls — yours, in your words, at the frequency you choose with them. Their replies come back to you. A fortnightly call becomes a fortnightly call plus a thread that never goes quiet.
  3. You see what the other services around them have logged — a referral made, an appointment attended, a discharge, an ADHD step reached — on that one person’s thread, to the extent each service has agreed to share. Never another service’s clinical notes. Never their record.
  4. You set your own signals. What counts as drift for your service — a missed reply, a missed session, three quiet days — is yours to define. When it trips, your named reviewer gets the alert. Nobody else decides for you.
  5. You keep a receipted thread for every person on it: what was sent, what was answered, what was handed on and whether it landed. Something you can point to in a review, or in front of a commissioner.

Everything else — the servers, the messaging, the consent model, the governance — is ours to carry. We are purely infrastructure. The people, the words and the decisions stay yours.

Hospital to community — where discharge meets follow-through. For CRS, the Layer is the community end of that line. Aftercare is voluntary by design; the onus is on the person to keep showing up — and for someone who has reached sober without the ADHD underneath ever being treated, showing up is exactly the thing their brain makes hardest. On the thread, community involvement becomes visible as involvement: a café attended, a creative recovery session, a wellbeing activity — a touchpoint logged, never the content. Nothing said in a group or a session is ever shared. And when the touchpoints stop, that is the signal: an alert to CRS, before crisis becomes consequence. CGL may begin the recovery turn. CRS helps it hold in the community. OTOS makes that continuity visible between them.

And the RCE Wellbeing Hub would be asked to contribute what it already offers — waiting well, diagnosis support, help with routine and daily life — carried through the week on the same thread. Personally, meditation, breathwork and a few other practices helped me and still do; what belongs on that list is theirs to decide.

Micro check-ins — what they actually are

Not reminders, and not an app watching anyone. Small, agreed SMS prompts — behaviour reinforcement, habit stacking — several a day, of varying kinds — built to replicate the intensity and repetition of residential rehab work, and carry it into an ordinary week. That is step three — the one that has nowhere to live. Think of it as an extension of what you already do: the connection your key workers make in a call, kept going in the days between calls. A little automated recovery — continuous connection — and, as we all know, connection is the opposite of addiction and a must-have in any successful recovery.

  • The person chooses the mix, and agrees it with their key worker. Not imposed, not clinical, not a monitoring app.
  • Every morning is a data point. A reply says someone is still there. Even a delivery receipt — the phone is on — is information you did not have yesterday.

What it does not do

  • Does not replace your clinical system or EPR, and stores no clinical records — only continuity signals and receipts
  • Does not add paperwork — one login, nothing to install, nothing else to learn
  • Does not make clinical decisions — every signal goes to a named human reviewer in your team
  • Does not require an NHS number — tokenised IDs by default. Not surveillance
  • Does not prioritise or fast-track anyone independently
We measure partner burden and treat it as a pre-pilot success criterion. If it takes too long, we have failed.

The design target is about 30–45 minutes a week per participating service during a live pre-pilot, tracked and adjusted if it turns out higher. A target to be tested, not a known quantity — nothing has run yet.

07

The evidence, and one example

ADHD in addiction is not a private hunch.

NICE’s own guideline tells clinicians to expect more ADHD among people with a history of substance misuse (NG87, rec 1.2.1), and names self-medication as a risk to discuss at diagnosis (rec 1.4.3). The Royal College of Psychiatrists says significant comorbid substance misuse should be managed jointly between adult and addiction services (CR235) — and, in the same guidance, says stabilise the substance misuse first and be cautious with stimulants in chaotic use. Both constraints are real, and they belong in the design, not the small print.

Your own organisation has published work on assessing ADHD inside substance-use services — the Nottinghamshire assessment pathway for adults using substances within the criminal justice system (Hammond, Murphy & Huke, Clinical Psychology Forum, November 2024; practice development, not a trial). CGL Nottingham is not OTOS proof. It is pathway proof. This is not me asking you to leap into the unknown. The recognition already exists inside your own world. And I should say it plainly: I came through CGL as a service user, I am writing to CGL now, and I am citing CGL’s own published work — all three at once.

It is retrospective, there is no control group, and I would never tell you it proves anything. It is what happened in one service in France, not what OTOS will do. But it is the shape of the thing I am trying to build: found, and held, so the recovery work can land.

And it is not only cost. Once my ADHD was treated, my own work rate did not just recover — it went up. Every cost model counts what a treated adult stops costing; none of them counts what he puts back. The avoided-cost side is the conservative side of the ledger.

What I am proposing needs a name, because without a named problem there is no named treatment. Neurologically Informed Addiction is founder-proposed terminology. It has not been submitted to any professional body and is not used anywhere else. It is my own framing — a way of looking at ADHD-driven addiction through a different lens instead of lumping it in with dual diagnosis or co-morbidity. The renaming is the re-understanding: how this cohort needs to be viewed, delivered and believed in.

Not ahead of the evidence. Precisely within it.

08

The plan — three stages

Each one earned by the one before it. Each one someone else’s to make.

  1. A research report — £4,950, one question. This one is mine to carry, not yours to think about yet: I am proposing it to Cambridgeshire Public Health as a first, small piece of commissioned analysis, to support what I am asking you for here. The question, exactly as it would be commissioned: “Is ADHD materially under-recognised among people at the intersection of high-impact use, addiction and mental-health complexity in Cambridgeshire — and what would the system need to know before deciding whether to act?” I can investigate, map and report on the local sequence of gaps, the handoffs where people go quiet, what a local cohort looks like from the services’ own view, and what the system would need to see to act. What it finds is the report’s to say, not mine to assume — and it has to be able to conclude that there is no signal.
  2. A bounded pre-pilot. If the answer is yes: small, time-limited, no new services onboarded. Participants pre-selected and loaded, each one consenting, the thread running for a fixed window, and everything measured — including your time. Scoped and costed, with the number under independent technical review; no figure travels until it has been.
  3. A pilot. Only if the pre-pilot holds. Someone else’s decision, made on what the first two stages actually showed.

The biggest upstream moment of all is at the hospital bedside, where people going through alcohol withdrawal can be reached before anything else has happened. That is a later stage, not this one — but it is where this goes.

Nobody is being asked to commit to stage two by looking at stage one. And the medical intervention is the point of all of it — getting people to assessment and treatment sooner, and holding them while they wait.

09

What I am asking you for

One thing, and it is small.

Your honest read on whether that first question is worth answering. If you think I am wrong, that is worth knowing, and I would far rather hear it from you now than find out later. And if you do think it is worth answering — saying so, in your own words, would carry more weight than anything I can write. You watch this happen every week. I am one person it happened to. That is the one thing I need your help with. Anything you say in support I would use for one purpose only, and only with your say-so: helping me put the research report in front of Cambridgeshire Public Health, so Stage 1 can get moving.

If the report shows what I think it will, the saving is not mine. It is money already being spent on repeat crisis that could go back into what you are good at as humans — more key workers, more contact, more of the thing that actually holds people. Continuity is the infrastructure underneath that. It is not the point; you are.

I am not asking CGL or CRS to approve, endorse or commit to anything. Not a pilot, not a referral route, not a change to how you work, and nothing that lands on your workload. The report has to be able to say there is nothing here. Research that can only reach one conclusion is not research.

I need help, and I would rather build this with the people who do the work than around them. If the report says the question is real, there is a version of what comes next where we do it together, and I would like that to be the version. I think it goes wider than this one cohort — bespoke continuity for complex cohorts across mental health and social prescribing — but ADHD-driven addiction is what I am tackling first, because it is mine.

I know there are people in your caseloads right now who cannot do what I did. They don’t have the resources, the language, the luck or the fight left in them. That is the gap OTOS Continuity™ is trying to close.

When we meet — what I would like to work through with you

  1. Does this match what you see? When someone cycles back through the door, how often is something like this underneath — and has there ever been anywhere to send them for it?
  2. The report. If Public Health funds one small piece of research on your cohort, what would make it useful to you rather than to me? What should it look at, and what should it leave alone?
  3. The shape. I have an ideal shape — a small, jointly agreed group, and a put-forward route into a clinically governed ADHD review: your criteria, your decision, every time. Give me the good, the bad and the ugly. Where does it break?
  4. Hospital to community. Where does the thread snap today — bedside to CGL, CGL to CRS — and what would you want to see on it if it didn’t?
  5. Your content. If the check-ins carried your programme’s own language, what would you want them to say between calls — and what must they never say?
  6. The honest one. Where would this get in the way of the work you already do?

I am not asking you to decide anything. I am asking you to help me get the question right before I take it to the people who can fund answering it.

I’ll finish by reconfirming how I feel — and it is awesome. It is literally a year ago that I got medicated, and my life changed. Thank you again for holding that thread.

The best thing, though, is the realisation that it appears my whole life story has been market research for this moment. I survived everything — I should be dead many times over. My journey is epic, but one prescription later I believe I have designed a system — phased, tested one stage at a time — that could help fix a national problem. I have done what I can. Now I need the next level of help — and a little of it from you.

You held the thread for me once. I am asking whether CGL can do it, on purpose, for the next person.
